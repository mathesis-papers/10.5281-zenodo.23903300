OEIS A209723: Barker's conjectures on distinct clockwise edge differences

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang ask how many rectangular grids can be filled with three symbols when every small square must have four different directed changes around its boundary. Their answer is an exact formula for every rectangular size, which proves Colin Barker’s conjectured formulas for five-column grids and several related sequences.

The symbols are the integers 0, 1 and 2. For each square formed by consecutive rows and columns, move clockwise and subtract each entry from the next, keeping negative signs rather than working modulo three. The four differences must be distinct. For example, top row 0, 1 and bottom row 2, 0 give differences 1, −1, 2, −2. How do these local restrictions constrain the whole grid? Let N(n,k) count grids with n+1 rows and k+1 columns, and let T(n,k) be that count divided by four. Positions and symbols remain distinguished: this is Hardin’s normalization, not identification under rotations or relabelling.

Define b(n) by interleaving two doubling sequences: its odd-position values are 2, 4, 8, …, and its even-position values are 3, 6, 12, …. The authors establish T(n,k) = b(n) + b(k) − 2 for all positive n and k. Each dimension contributes separately, despite the overlapping constraints. Five columns means k=4, so A209723 is b(n)+4, beginning 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 16. This proves Barker’s July 12, 2018 parity formulas and ordinary generating function, a formal power series recording the counts. It also proves Hardin’s empirical recurrence: from the fourth term onward, each term equals the previous term plus twice the second previous term minus twice the third previous term.

The proof first classifies small squares. Their differences lie between −2 and 2 and sum to zero, so four distinct choices must be exactly −2, −1, 1 and 2. The edges carrying 2 and −2 cannot be opposite, because the remaining differences would coincide. They must meet, forcing the remaining corner to carry 1; altogether eight squares are permissible. For a two-row grid, regard each vertical column as a state. Permissible transitions form two separate three-state stars: 02 connects to 10 and 21, while 20 connects to 01 and 12. A star has a centre joined to two leaves, with no connection between leaves.

For arbitrary width, the authors instead make whole rows the states, connecting two whenever they can occur consecutively. A grid corresponds exactly to a walk through this graph, with its starting row specified and revisits allowed. Only the alternating rows 0202… and 2020… can have two neighbours. Any other usable row contains 1; an adjacent pair involving 1 uniquely fixes the corresponding lower pair. Column transitions propagate that choice uniquely in both directions. Thus the usable row graph consists of two three-vertex stars and otherwise isolated edges. Counting two-row grids by columns shows that there are exactly 2b(k)−4 isolated edges.

This structure explains the formula. Within a star, walks alternate between centre and leaves; each two-step extension doubles the choices, producing the odd/even pattern. The two stars supply 4b(n) walks of length n. On an isolated edge, every step is forced after choosing the starting endpoint, so it contributes exactly two walks of any positive length. Adding these contributions and dividing by four gives the answer. The array-to-walk correspondence is standard; the decisive feature here is the explicit classification of the graph’s components.

The manuscript credits Andrew’s 2024 tile-based proof outline for four columns; the authors’ written argument handles all widths together. It yields the companion formulas in A209721–A209726, every column recurrence in A209727, and that table’s diagonal identification with A027383, starting at n=1. For three columns it proves Beck’s first conjecture and Peterson’s recurrence and integer-triangle interpretation: from the third term onward, fixing the preceding two values as side lengths leaves exactly the next value’s worth of integer third sides, by the strict triangle inequalities.

The all-size conclusion rests on the written proof, not finite enumeration. The authors report checks of all 81 candidate squares, graph structure through width 12, and tabulated sequence values. They describe the linked Lean declaration as covering only the five-column parity identities, recurrence, generating-series existence and denominator identity—not the all-width theorem or other corollaries. This describes evidence rather than independently certifying it. The result concerns positive n,k and signed integer differences only. Beck’s second A209721 conjecture, about zero positions in a difference of sequences, and Reverter i Bigas’s A209724 smallest-integer/divisor conjecture remain outside its coverage.
